The record

Most job boards cannot tell you who sponsors. Public filings can.

Every employer that sponsors a work visa leaves a paper trail with the Department of Labor and USCIS. SponsorJobs reads that trail, matches it to 1,375 employer job boards, and puts the mark next to the job. No survey, no guess, no "visa friendly" badge an employer paid for.

MarkWhat it meansWhy it matters to youSource
H-1B
The employer has approved H-1B petitions on record in recent fiscal years.
They have done this before. Their HR and counsel know the process, and they budget for it.
USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub, DOL LCA disclosure data
Green Card
The employer has certified PERM labor applications, the first step to an employer-sponsored green card.
The strongest signal. They have sponsored people all the way to permanent residence.
DOL PERM disclosure data

Required for the 24-month STEM OPT extension. Without it, your STEM extension cannot be approved at that employer.

Filings lag by a few months and are made per employer, not per team or per job. A mark means the employer has sponsored, not that this manager will. It is still the best public evidence there is.

01Find

Only roles at employers with a record.

Jobs come straight from 1,375 employer career sites and applicant tracking systems, refreshed every few hours. Each one carries the employer's sponsorship marks, the pay compared with similar roles, and the visa classes that employer has filed for by nationality.

  • FilterSponsorship, entry level, pay, type, posted date. Save a search and get a daily count of new sponsor roles.
  • FitOnce your resume is in, every posting shows which of its must-have skills you cover and which you do not.
  • HonestPostings that say "no sponsorship" are flagged before you read them.
02Tailor

One page. Every time. In the job's own words.

Pick a template, and SponsorJobs rewrites your real experience toward the job description. It uses the posting's vocabulary where your material supports it, keeps the LaTeX layout to one page, and shows which required skills you cover and which you do not. It never adds a skill you do not have.

  • LayoutTypeset in LaTeX. If a rewrite would spill to page two, it shortens and recompiles until it fits.
  • CoverageA report per job: what the posting asks for, what is on your resume, and the gap between them.
  • TemplatesSixteen single-column templates that applicant tracking systems can parse.
03Apply

It fills the form. Who clicks Submit depends on the site.

Each approved job becomes a package: tailored resume, a short cover letter, and screening answers drawn from answers you gave once. Then one of two things happens, and the queue tells you which.

  • Sent for youOnly where the site has a verified, keyless application API. Today that is Recruitee. Off by default; you switch it on.
  • Your clickEverywhere else, including Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and LinkedIn. We fill the form in the app's own browser pane; you review and press Submit.
  • NeverNo LinkedIn automation. No captcha tricks, no fake identities, no getting around a site's bot rules. If a site says no, we stop and hand it to you.
04Practise

Rehearse the interview for this job, not an interview in general.

Round one is a recorded screen: five timed questions on camera, built from the posting and your resume, scored afterwards. Pass it and round two opens: twenty minutes with a live AI interviewer who has read the same job description you did.

  • BriefingBefore you start: likely questions, what to mention, and what the posting says about the team.
  • Live roundWorks out loud without a camera too. Three live interviews come with a Job Hunt Pass, nine with a Season Pass.
  • On the dayNothing runs during your real interview. This is practice, not a hidden earpiece.

Your data stays here.

Your profile, resumes and applications live in a folder on your computer. There is no SponsorJobs server that holds them, and we cannot see them.

Text goes to the AI provider you choose, with your own key, or through our metered broker on a pass. Either way it is used to write, then discarded. We keep a usage count for billing and nothing else.

The shared job list contains no personal data. It is public postings plus public government records. Your profile is never sent to it.

Install

Two things first, then three steps.

Before you install

LaTeX. SponsorJobs typesets your resume with it, so it has to be on the machine. The download page gives the one command for each system.

An AI key, community edition only. From Anthropic or OpenAI, if you run from source. The installed app needs no key: the AI is included.

macOS Apple Silicon

  1. Install LaTeX. In Terminal: brew install --cask mactex-no-gui
  2. Download the .dmg, open it, and drag SponsorJobs to Applications.
  3. On first open, macOS says it cannot verify the developer. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway. Once.

The app is not yet signed with an Apple Developer certificate, which is why macOS asks. The source is public, so you can check what you are opening.

Windows 10 or later, 64-bit

  1. Install LaTeX: MiKTeX. Let it install missing packages on the fly.
  2. Download SponsorJobs-Setup.exe and run it.
  3. SmartScreen may warn that the publisher is unknown. Click More info, then Run anyway.

The installer is not yet code-signed, which is why SmartScreen asks.

Download page, with version, size and date of the current release. Linux, Intel Macs, or you would rather build it yourself: the README covers it in about ten minutes.
